Lithium-ion batteries are now everywhere: in electric vehicles, energy storage systems, tools, scooters, and consumer electronics. Warehouses that store them in bulk carry a fire risk profile that differs from typical storage. When fire protection at these sites is impaired, or when hot work is underway, fire watch has to account for that difference. ## Why Battery Storage Isn't Just Another Warehouse

In a typical warehouse, the main fuels are cardboard, plastic, and wood pallets. Those burn hot, but they behave in familiar ways. Lithium-ion batteries add a different mechanism: thermal runaway, where a damaged or defective cell heats uncontrollably, can release flammable and toxic gases, and can spread to neighboring cells. Battery fires can be hard to extinguish and may reignite after appearing to be out.

That has practical consequences for fire watch. Early signs may include smoke or vapor from a pallet, unusual heat, hissing or popping sounds, or a sweet or chemical odor. Damaged packaging, swollen cells, or recently dropped pallets deserve attention. And the response priorities are clear: alert, call 911, evacuate, and follow the site's emergency plan, rather than attempting heroics on a battery fire.

## The Components of Battery Warehouse Fire Watch

**Trigger conditions:** Sprinkler or detection impairments (NFPA 25, NFPA 72, IFC 901.7), hot work during maintenance or construction (OSHA 1910.252, NFPA 51B), construction phases (NFPA 241), insurer impairment requirements, and AHJ orders.

**Site-specific briefing:** Battery storage locations, damaged-battery quarantine areas, charging areas, exits, alarm activation points, and the site's emergency response plan.

**Patrol focus:** Racks and aisles holding battery stock, quarantine and returns areas, charging stations, receiving docks, and areas where hot work is underway.

**Escalation:** Clear instructions on when to call 911, who on site to notify, and when to evacuate.

**Documentation:** Every round logged with time, areas checked, and observations, ideally GPS-verified.

## Why Staffing Matters More at Battery Sites

Battery warehouses are often large, and early detection is the main defense while protection is impaired. Staffing should match the affected area and the patrol interval the AHJ or insurer requires. Quarantine and charging areas may justify more frequent checks. A provider's staffing team should confirm the impairment type, local fire department, timeline, and guard count before quoting.

## Housekeeping That Supports the Fire Watch

Fire watch works better when the site reduces risk at the same time. Keep damaged or returned batteries in their designated quarantine area. Keep aisles clear so the guard can see and reach every rack. Limit charging activity during the impairment where possible. And keep combustible packaging away from battery stock and hot work areas.

## What Realistic Coverage Looks Like

Coverage typically starts as soon as protection is impaired, runs continuously through shift rotations, and ends only when systems are restored, tested, and released by the AHJ. For hot work, the watch continues at least 30 minutes after work ends under OSHA 1910.252, longer if the permit requires.
